# ADR-0003 — Patient & Clinical Record Domain Model

**Status:** Accepted
**Project:** Lipi
**Sprint:** Sprint 2 — Patient & Clinical Record Domain Model

## Executive Summary

Sprint 2 defines the minimum patient and clinical-record concepts required for the MVP of Lipi.

The sprint deliberately builds upon the architectural decisions established in Sprint 1 rather than redefining them. In particular, the consultation workflow and the broad responsibilities of the Patient Domain were already established in ADR-0002.

The objective of Sprint 2 was therefore to clarify the information Lipi must preserve and present to a doctor during the MVP workflow without introducing unnecessary clinical modelling.

Lipi will prioritize preservation and accessibility of the doctor's records rather than attempting to interpret, summarize, or make clinical decisions from them.

---

## Patient

A Patient represents an individual receiving care through the clinic.

### Minimum Patient Information

The MVP requires:

* Name
* Age
* Gender
* Height
* Weight

These constitute the minimum identifying and basic physical information required by the current MVP workflow.

### Clinical Background

The patient record should also provide access to relevant prior medical information, including:

* Previous medical conditions and history
* Previous symptoms
* Medication history
* Allergy information

Allergy information is optional to provide but is considered clinically important and must therefore be supported by Lipi.

---

## Patient Discovery

Patients are accessed through the **Patient Database**.

The primary mechanism for locating an existing patient is the **Search** function.

The MVP does not introduce additional patient-discovery mechanisms beyond this requirement.

---

## Clinical History

For the purposes of the MVP, a patient's clinical history consists of their relevant prior medical information and previously recorded clinical material.

When reviewing a patient's previous history, the doctor should be able to access:

* Previous prescriptions written for the patient
* Scan and investigation reports associated with the patient

Lipi's responsibility is to preserve and present these records.

It does not attempt to determine which information is medically significant.

---

## Consultation

The definition and workflow of a Consultation were already established during Sprint 1 and are therefore not redefined by this sprint.

The established clinical workflow is:

> Identify patient → Listen → Review history → Examine → Diagnose → Explain → Write prescription → Decide follow-up → Save consultation.

The consultation remains part of the patient's longitudinal clinical record.

Sprint 2 does not introduce additional consultation concepts beyond what is necessary for the MVP.

---

## Doctor Decision-Making

Lipi will not interpret or summarize clinical records for the doctor in the MVP.

The application follows the architectural principle established in Sprint 1:

> **Doctor decides. Assist, never assume.**

Lipi provides access to the patient's records and preserves the doctor's documentation. It does not attempt to determine diagnoses, prioritize clinical information, or provide clinical recommendations.

---

## MVP Domain Model

The resulting conceptual model is intentionally small:

```text
Patient
│
├── Patient Information
│
├── Clinical History
│
└── Consultation History
       │
       ├── Previous Prescriptions
       │
       └── Scan / Investigation Reports
```

The Consultation workflow remains governed by the architecture established in ADR-0002.

---

## Deliberately Excluded from This Sprint

The following were intentionally not modelled because they are either implementation concerns, future capabilities, or unnecessary for the MVP:

* Database schema
* SQLite or other storage technology
* `.lipi` file structure
* Encryption
* Cloud synchronization
* AI-based interpretation
* Hospital workflows
* Detailed medical ontology
* Automated clinical summarization
* Clinical decision support
* Additional entities introduced solely for architectural completeness

These may be reconsidered in future development if actual requirements justify them.

---

## Design Principle

The MVP domain model should remain as simple as the real workflow permits.

Lipi should not introduce complexity merely because a concept could theoretically be represented as a separate entity or subsystem.

The application exists to support the doctor's workflow, not to impose a software-generated model of clinical practice.

---

## Conclusion

Sprint 2 establishes the minimum domain model required for Lipi's MVP.

The Patient record provides the doctor's essential identifying information and relevant clinical background. Patients are located through the Patient Database's search functionality. Previous prescriptions and scan/investigation reports constitute the primary historical clinical material that must be accessible during review.

The Consultation workflow remains governed by the architecture established in Sprint 1.

No further domain modelling is required for the current MVP.

Future domain expansion should be driven by demonstrated clinical requirements rather than speculative architectural needs.
